Bentonite works because of montmorillonite: aluminosilicate sheets one nanometre thick, stacked like pages. Water slips between them and the clay swells. Every grade we sell is controlled from that layer up.

Anatomy of a grain

Three sheets, then water. Repeat.

Each montmorillonite layer is a sandwich: two silica sheets around one alumina sheet. Sodium ions sit between the layers and pull water in. That is the swelling buyers pay for, and it is why we keep sodium and calcium clay apart from the face onwards.

Mine to vessel

Five controlled transitions

01

Selective mining

Sodium and calcium horizons worked separately, sampled at the face before a truck is loaded.

02

Yard blending

Lots combined to the order's swelling index and methylene-blue value.

03

Drying & activation

Rotary drying to 10–12% moisture; soda-ash exchange turns calcium sites into sodium sites where a grade needs it.

04

Milling

Raymond and micronising mills to 200, 325 or 400 mesh, with on-line sieve checks.

05

Packing & shipment

Bags, jumbo bags or bulk liners, each lot with a certificate of analysis and a retained sample.
